# Margin Enterprise rewrite notes

Source: `/home/ubuntu/upload/Margin_Enterprise_page_hard_rewrite.pdf`

## Strategic direction

The page must stop sounding like a technical access tier. It should position Margin as **the recovery-control layer for high-GMV Amazon businesses**. The buyer is not purchasing infrastructure or server capacity; they are purchasing a more reliable way to identify, prove, prioritize, and own marketplace leakage across complex operations.

The page must **not** claim dedicated infrastructure, priority API limits, isolated workers, custom API integrations, or certification unless those capabilities are already live, documented, and contractually supportable.

### Section 1 — The problem at enterprise scale
- Section title: **The more you sell, the less you can afford to leave unresolved.**
- Positioning: At $1M+ GMV, discrepancies stop being small. Inventory moves across accounts, markets, warehouses, SKUs, and reporting periods. Reimbursements are approved, reversed, offset, or paid on different timelines.
- Consequence line: **The result is not only missed recovery. It is poor financial visibility, duplicated work, slow close, and decisions made from incomplete evidence.**
- Table intro: **Margin helps your team answer five questions:**
- Table rows captured:
  - What happened? → The transaction, inventory, case, payout, or reversal trail.
  - What is financially material? → The amount and operational significance of the unresolved issue.
  - What proves it? → The source records and evidence supporting the finding.
  - What is still incomplete? → The coverage limitation, missing record, or unresolved uncertainty.
  - What happens next? → The accountable route: recover, investigate, remediate, monitor, or close.

### Editorial rules
The Enterprise page may use corporate and technical language, but every abstract term must be tied to a buyer consequence. Technical nouns alone are not persuasive to the high-GMV buyer. The page should avoid unverified claims and fake enterprise signals. Logos, certifications, priority infrastructure, dedicated nodes, global coverage, custom integrations, and guaranteed turnaround claims should appear only when Margin can substantiate them in diligence.
